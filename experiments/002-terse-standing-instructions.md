# Experiment 002 — terse standing instructions and a stop condition

**Status:** active field test  
**Start date:** 2026-09-22

## Question

Can a shorter, more declarative standing prompt produce more noticeable behavior in ordinary use, especially by suppressing a recurring tendency to append unsolicited continuations after the request is already complete?

Experiment 001 used three explanatory paragraphs and produced no clearly memorable ecological effect. Experiment 002 preserves the same general direction while compressing it into four independently actionable rules.

## Treatment

Use the following text unchanged as the standing user preference:

> Treat chat as scratch space; preserve consequential state in durable artifacts.
>
> Preserve convergence. Reopen settled choices only for a concrete defect, contradiction, or new evidence.
>
> Use your capabilities aggressively in service of my goals; keep substantive decisions and handoffs legible.
>
> Answer the request at hand and stop when it is complete.

## Why this version

The change is partly about content and partly about form.

The first three rules compress Experiment 001 rather than introducing a new collaboration theory:

- chat is useful working memory, not durable project state;
- convergence should have inertia once earned;
- delegation is welcome, but important decisions and handoffs should remain visible.

The fourth rule is new and deliberately sharp:

> Answer the request at hand and stop when it is complete.

A recurring behavioral motif in recent ChatGPT use has been an unsolicited bounded continuation: answer the request, then offer a three-part plan, readiness check, next exercise, or explicit continuation. That behavior has been more salient than the previous user-preference treatment.

The stop condition therefore serves as a black-box probe of instruction hierarchy: can a terse user preference push against a strong recurring style tendency?

## What to notice

Do not manufacture a test battery yet. Use ChatGPT normally.

Record only salient cases where:

- a response reaches a natural endpoint and stops cleanly;
- a response still appends an unsolicited continuation after the task is complete;
- consequential state is surfaced for preservation without ceremony;
- a settled choice is usefully protected or wrongly frozen;
- a substantive decision or handoff becomes more legible;
- the terse prompt causes obvious annoyance, timidity, or brittleness.

The primary behavioral target is the stop condition. The other three rules remain ecological observations.

## Interpretation

This is still a field test, not a causal identification strategy.

If the continuation motif becomes much less common, that is evidence that user-level preferences can meaningfully steer at least one conspicuous tendency. It would not identify whether the tendency originated in the model, harness, post-training, or conversation history.

If the motif continues to punch through, that is also interesting, but still ambiguous: the user-level rule may be weak, inconsistently applied, context-dependent, or competing with stronger upstream pressures.

The other three clauses are retained because they remain desirable working hypotheses, but this experiment does not pretend to isolate their individual effects.

## Stopping rule

Run this as an ordinary-use prompt rather than continually editing it.

Revisit after roughly a week, or earlier only if a strong failure mode or conspicuous behavioral effect appears.
